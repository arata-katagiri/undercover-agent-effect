## Interpretation

Direct and indirect solicitations changed immediate transfers to the organizer by +0.750 and +0.750, respectively, relative to matched no-message controls when the organizer kept its promise.
After explicit solicitation, public betrayal changed later organizer-directed transfers by -0.393 and retaliation by +0.643. This measures behavioral adaptation, not an internal intention to collude.

## Limitations

- This was an exploratory screen with prompt-defined roles, one model, one payoff table, and a small number of fresh seeds per cell.
- A role prompt induces behavior; it is not evidence that an unprompted model naturally possesses that stable personality or strategy.
- Correct-report labels use receipt of an organizer's solicitation as ground truth. They do not prove that the accused agent had a durable hidden intention.
- The direct message is more explicit than the indirect message. Differences combine social framing, information content, and requested action specificity.
- Transfers are positive-sum, so discriminatory cooperation and social harm are distinct.
- No null-hypothesis p-values are reported for this exploratory matrix. Fresh confirmatory seeds and a reduced comparison set are needed before inferential claims.
